# Hand-sell venues (Broward County)

`BUSINESS_PLAN.md` phase 5 — "hand-selling 30–50 units" — has never had any
concrete research behind it until now. This is a first pass at *where*
that could actually happen, not a finished plan; none of these have been
contacted or confirmed to accept cottage food vendors.

## Why this matters now
Legal formation (Broward, confirmed) and the recipe test protocol give a
path to an actual product. Hand-selling is the very next real-world step
after that — but "sell directly" was never grounded in real venues/events.
This closes that gap.

## Candidate farmers markets / vendor events in Broward County
Found via web search, none contacted or fee-confirmed yet:
- **Yellow Green Farmers Market** — one of the larger, longer-running
  South Florida markets.
- **Las Olas Oceanside Park Market** — Fort Lauderdale beachfront market.
- **Plantation Farmer's Market** — runs year-round per Community Farmers
  Markets of South Florida.
- **Green Market Pompano Beach**
- **Coconut Creek Hometown Market**
- **Parkland Farmers' Market** — seasonal, returns Nov 1 each year.
- **Lauderdale-by-the-Sea Market** — seasonal, Sundays Dec–May, 9am–1pm at
  El Prado Park; explicitly features "artisans and local vendors" with
  baked goods, jellies, and crafts — a good stylistic fit for a small
  craft candy brand.
- **Pop Up & Shop Up** — runs one-off pop-up markets (e.g. at Atlantic
  Village) for "handcrafted goods... small brands" — worth watching for
  dates that fit a launch window rather than committing to a recurring
  market.

A dedicated cottage-food-specific market directory exists at
floridacottagefoods.com/farmers-markets, but it returned an access error
to automated fetch (same 403 pattern as other government/directory sites
in this repo's research) — worth a human visiting it directly for a more
complete, cottage-food-specific list.

## What's not yet known (needs a human to actually contact these)
- Vendor application process and fees — varies by market, not found in
  this pass.
- Whether each market's rules accommodate a cottage food operation
  specifically (some farmers markets are produce-only, or have separate
  tiers for prepared foods).
- Table/booth setup requirements (some require insurance certificates —
  ties back to the open insurance decision in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`; a
  market requiring proof of liability coverage could force that decision
  rather than leaving it open).

## Non-market alternative: direct/social hand-selling
Given the hand-sell phase is only 30–50 units, a farmers market booth may
be more infrastructure than needed. Simpler alternatives worth weighing
against a formal market booth:
- Direct sales to friends/family/local network — zero venue cost, fastest
  to start, smallest reach.
- A simple pre-order post on personal or a new brand social account,
  with local pickup — no booth fee, tests real demand before committing to
  market logistics.
- One-off local pop-up/event (per Pop Up & Shop Up above) as a single
  concentrated selling moment, rather than a recurring market commitment.

Recommendation: given the small first-batch size and that a market
vendor fee/insurance requirement could eat into the already-tight $500
budget, a direct/social approach is probably the lower-risk way to hit the
first 30–50 units, with a single well-chosen pop-up or market appearance
(if one requires no insurance and low/no fee) as a stretch option — not a
prerequisite.
