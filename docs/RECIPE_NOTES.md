# Recipe notes (non-binding, needs real kitchen testing)

Starting-point ratios only — nothing here has been tested. Treat as a first
draft to bring into actual test batches, not a final formulation.

## Base ratio (per standard chocolate-bar formulation ranges)
- Cacao butter + cacao solids: ~60–70% of total weight (craft dark-chocolate
  range).
- Sweetener: ~15–35% by weight (dark-chocolate range; King & Sepal's melt
  use case may want the higher end for better dissolve/sweetness once
  diluted into a drink).
- Lecithin (soy or sunflower): ~0.4–1.4% of the fat phase — this is the
  piece that makes the "fully dissolve in coffee" target achievable at all;
  see `RESEARCH.md`. Needs to be food-grade and ideally non-GMO/allergen
  labeled clearly per the FL cottage-food labeling requirement.
- Flavoring: added on top, varies by flavor (see below).

## Per-flavor starting points
- **French Vanilla**: real vanilla bean/extract + a touch of milk powder or
  cream flavoring, to read as "vanilla latte" once melted, not just vanilla
  chocolate.
- **Caramel**: caramelized sugar or caramel flavoring worked into the
  sweetener component, plus a pinch of salt to keep it from reading as flat.
- **Hazelnut**: hazelnut paste/praline blended in — watch that this doesn't
  push the fat content too high (hazelnut paste is itself fat-heavy), which
  would fight against the "fully dissolve, no residue" target rather than
  help it.

## Open testing questions
- Does dissolve behavior differ meaningfully between black coffee, coffee
  with milk, and tea? (Coffee-creamer literature suggests emulsifiers
  perform differently across those.)
- Shelf life / melting point at Florida ambient/shipping temperatures —
  cocoa butter's melt point (~93–101°F) is close enough to a hot car or
  un-air-conditioned porch that shipping stability needs real testing, not
  just an estimate.
- Texture as a straight candy bar (eat-side) vs. as a melt (drink-side) may
  pull the recipe in different directions — worth testing both uses
  explicitly rather than assuming one recipe nails both.
