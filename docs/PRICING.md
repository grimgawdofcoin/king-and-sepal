# Pricing (proposed, not decided)

Not in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` as a hard blocker yet, but flagged as open in
`BRAND.md`. This is a researched recommendation for the founder to confirm
or override — not a final decision.

## Market reference points
Craft/bean-to-bar chocolate (Dandelion, Fruition, Pump Street, Dick Taylor,
etc.) retails around **$7–16 per bar** individually, or **$40–80 per
shipment** through direct-to-consumer subscription/club programs (4–6 bars
per box). Source: JayArr Chocolate craft-chocolate subscription roundup,
cross-checked against maker sites (dandelionchocolate.com,
fruitionchocolateworks.com, pumpstreetchocolate.com).

## Where King & Sepal sits
King & Sepal isn't directly comparable to a standard craft bar — it's a
dual-use product (candy + drink creamer) with no direct packaged-goods
competitor, sold through an exclusive-drop model rather than always-on
retail. That combination supports pricing at or above the top of the craft
range, not in the middle:
- **Per-bar**: suggest **$12–18**, above typical bean-to-bar pricing,
  justified by the novelty of the melt mechanism and the drop scarcity —
  but not so high it reads as a gimmick tax with no substance behind it.
- **Hand-sell phase (30–50 units)**: consider pricing at the low end of
  that range, or bundling (e.g. a 3-flavor sampler), since the goal here is
  word-of-mouth and feedback, not margin.
- **Drop 01**: once validated, price can move toward the top of the range
  or introduce a small-batch/limited-run premium tier, consistent with the
  Ffern-style scarcity positioning.

## What this doesn't account for yet
- Actual per-unit cost (cacao, lecithin, flavoring, packaging, shipping) —
  once a real cacao-butter quote and a packaging-direction decision land,
  margin can be checked against these numbers instead of just market
  comparables.
- Florida cottage food sales-tax handling on direct sales.
