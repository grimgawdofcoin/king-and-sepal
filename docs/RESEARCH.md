### Melt-in-drink precedent
- "Coffee bombs": chocolate shells filled with instant coffee/creamer,
  dissolve when hot water is poured over them — an established DIY/recipe
  trend, not yet a major branded packaged-goods category.
  (https://amagicalmess.com/mocha-coffee-bombs/)
- Melting a chocolate bar/morsel directly into espresso is a known
  trend/technique (https://www.mashed.com/1192649/yes-you-can-melt-a-chocolate-bar-into-your-espresso/).
- Candy-flavored *liquid* coffee creamers (Kit Kat, Twix, Reese's, Hershey's
  Caramel) are an active, growing category from major brands (Coffee Mate,
  International Delight) — validates consumer appetite for "candy in your
  coffee," but all existing versions are liquid, not a solid bar you also
  eat as candy. (https://www.trendhunter.com/trends/kit-kat-creamer)

### Manufacturing feasibility
Cocoa-butter-based "fast melt" tablets are a studied formulation approach:
cocoa butter blended with waxes/PEG 6000/starch to control melt behavior,
per food-science literature (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9143478/).
Keto "fat bomb" recipes (cocoa butter + coconut oil + sweetener, molded and
set) are a close DIY analog for a bar meant to melt cleanly into a hot
liquid. This suggests the core mechanism is achievable without exotic
technology, but needs real recipe development and testing (does it dissolve
fully without residue, foam/curdle in coffee vs. tea vs. milk, hold up to
shipping in a hot climate, etc.) before committing to a formulation.

## 2026-07-11 — Florida cottage food + sourcing + "fully dissolve"

### Florida cottage food law
Florida allows home-kitchen food production with **no license, permit, or
state inspection**, up to **$250,000/yr gross sales** (calendar year,
resets Jan 1) — the highest cap of any state. Candy, fudge, chocolate-covered
items, caramel, and confections generally are an allowed category. Required
on every package: business name/address, product name, ingredients in
descending weight order, net weight/volume, allergens, and the verbatim
disclaimer "Made in a cottage food operation that is not subject to
Florida's food safety regulations" (≥10-point type). Allowed: in-state
online/social/Etsy sales, in-state shipping. Not allowed: wholesale to
stores/restaurants, interstate shipping, institutional sales (schools,
hospitals). Source: Fla. Stat. § 500.80
(https://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599%2F0500%2FSections%2F0500.80.html),
cross-checked against https://standscout.com/blog/florida-cottage-food-laws-2026
and https://floridacottagefoods.com/sell-from-home/legal-checklist.
This directly supports the founder's home-kitchen decision — no permit
needed to start, but interstate shipping (i.e. selling outside Florida) is
off the table until moving to a licensed commercial kitchen/co-packer.

### "Fully dissolve" formulation note
Plain cocoa butter is a fat — it melts but does not dissolve in a
water-based liquid like coffee; on its own it will separate/float rather
than disperse. Food-industry coffee-creamer formulations solve this with a
**lecithin emulsifier** (soy or sunflower lecithin, typically ~1–5% of the
oil phase), which lets the fat disperse into hot coffee without separating
or "feathering," even in coffee's acidic environment. This means the King &
Sepal recipe needs lecithin (or a comparable food-grade emulsifier) blended
in, not just cocoa butter + flavoring, to actually hit the "fully dissolve,
no residue" target. Sources:
https://mtroyal.com.tr/en/lecithin-for-coffee-creamers/,
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0260877417301516.
