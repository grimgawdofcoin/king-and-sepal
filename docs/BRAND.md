# Brand concept

## The product

A solid cacao-based bar with two uses:
1. **Eat it** like a candy bar.
2. **Melt it** — drop it in hot coffee, tea, or milk, where it dissolves into a
   flavored creamer.

Launch flavors under discussion: **French Vanilla**, **Caramel**. Candidates to
consider later: mocha, cinnamon, hazelnut, salted caramel.

This is a real, buildable product: it sits at the intersection of two known
categories — "coffee bomb" melts (chocolate shells that dissolve in hot liquid)
and cocoa-butter fast-melt tablets (studied in food-science literature: cocoa
butter + coconut oil/waxes formulated to melt cleanly at drink temperature).
See [`RESEARCH.md`](RESEARCH.md) for sources. No direct competitor was found
selling this as a craft, exclusive-drop candy/creamer hybrid — that's the gap.

## Positioning: the Ffern model

[Ffern](https://ffern.co) is a UK natural-perfume brand built entirely on
scarcity and restraint:
- A waitlist ("the ledger") gates access — reportedly ~500,000 people deep.
- One seasonal batch, made to order for ledger members only, four times a year.
- No aggressive marketing, no constant new-product churn — the scarcity and
  the ritual *are* the marketing.
- Every shipment is a considered unboxing experience (compostable packaging,
  original artwork), not just a product.

King & Sepal borrows the shape of this, not the fragrance industry specifics:
- Small, numbered **drops** instead of always-on inventory.
- Early traction through **hand-selling** (30–50 units) before any paid
  marketing — proof the product earns word of mouth before scaling spend.
- **Drop 01** as the first real public release, sized and paced deliberately
  rather than "launch and hope."
- Packaging and unboxing treated as part of the product, not an afterthought.
